This is an estimate, not tax advice. It uses 2026 federal brackets and standard deductions (IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32), the 2026 Social Security wage base, and each state's 2026 income-tax schedule. It does not include tax credits (child, EITC, education), state-specific deductions or exemptions, local/city income taxes, the QBI deduction, capital-gains rates, AMT, above-the-line adjustments, or the 2025 to 2028 deductions for tips, overtime, seniors, and car-loan interest. Married-joint state figures use each state's joint brackets; head-of-household and married-separate use the single schedule. Pre-tax retirement is treated as traditional (pre-tax); a percentage applies to base and bonus and stops at the $24,500 limit, and age 50+ catch-up ($8,000, or $11,250 at ages 60 to 63) needs a dollar amount and HSA as a payroll contribution; a few states differ (PA taxes 401(k) contributions; CA and NJ tax HSA contributions) and that isn't modeled. A bonus is taxed as ordinary income at your normal brackets; only the withholding differs. The bonus breakdown assumes your employer uses the IRS flat percentage method for supplemental wages (22% federal, 37% on supplemental pay above $1 million in a year) rather than the aggregate method, that it's your only supplemental pay this year, and that withholding on your base pay is already accurate. Some states withhold bonuses at their own flat supplemental rate; the breakdown shows the state tax the bonus actually adds. Verify with a professional or the IRS/state site before filing.
